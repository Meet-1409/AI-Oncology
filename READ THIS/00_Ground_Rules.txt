00_Ground_Rules.txt

Project Name

AI Oncology Patient Intelligence Platform

Project Purpose

This document defines the permanent rules of the project. Every developer, designer, AI model, and contributor must follow these rules throughout development. These rules are the highest priority and cannot be overridden unless the Product Owner explicitly changes them.

--------------------------------------------------

1. Project Vision

1.1 The platform is built to help oncologists understand a patient's complete cancer journey.

1.2 The platform assists doctors and never replaces their medical judgement.

1.3 Every feature must solve a real clinical problem.

1.4 Every feature must improve the workflow of either the patient or the oncologist.

1.5 The platform is experienced as a continuous environment, not as a collection of pages.

1.6 The platform must look and feel years ahead of existing healthcare software while remaining practical for daily clinical use.

--------------------------------------------------

2. User Roles

2.1 There are only two user roles.

- Patient
- Oncologist

2.2 No other doctors or healthcare professionals will use the platform directly.

2.3 Every patient is assigned to one primary oncologist.

--------------------------------------------------

3. Patient Case

3.1 Everything belongs to a Patient Case.

3.2 Every report remains linked to the Patient Case.

3.3 Every AI result remains linked to the Patient Case.

3.4 Every note remains linked to the Patient Case.

3.5 Every task remains linked to the Patient Case.

3.6 Every timeline event remains linked to the Patient Case.

3.7 Patient history must never be deleted.

--------------------------------------------------

4. Patient Reports

4.1 Patients upload their own reports.

4.2 Reports may come from any hospital, laboratory or diagnostic centre.

4.3 The platform is independent of hospitals.

4.4 Original reports must always be preserved.

4.5 Reports must never be modified.

4.6 Previous reports must always remain accessible.

--------------------------------------------------

5. Artificial Intelligence

5.1 AI is an assistant.

5.2 AI never replaces the oncologist.

5.3 AI organizes patient information.

5.4 AI summarizes reports.

5.5 AI compares reports.

5.6 AI builds the patient's clinical timeline.

5.7 AI must only use validated patient data.

5.8 AI must never create information that does not exist.

5.9 Every AI output must display supporting evidence.

5.10 Every AI output must display confidence.

5.11 Doctors must always be able to verify AI outputs.

--------------------------------------------------

6. Digital Twin

6.1 Every patient must have an interactive Digital Twin.

6.2 The Digital Twin is a clinical visualization.

6.3 The Digital Twin is generated using patient information and available medical data.

6.4 The Digital Twin is not an exact copy of the patient's appearance.

6.5 The Digital Twin should represent the patient's body structure using available patient information.

6.6 Cancer should be displayed on the correct organs whenever supported by the available data.

6.7 Disease severity should be represented using different shades of red.

6.8 Disease progression should be visible across time.

6.9 Doctors must be able to rotate the model.

6.10 Doctors must be able to zoom the model.

6.11 Doctors must be able to pan the model.

6.12 Camera movement must never change medical information.

6.13 Transitions between different clinical dates must be smooth.

6.14 Previous and current patient states must be comparable side by side.

6.15 The Digital Twin is the centrepiece of the application and one of its primary navigation spaces.

6.16 The Digital Twin integrates directly with reports, timeline and patient intelligence.

6.17 Selecting an organ must reveal the clinical information and supporting evidence related to that organ.

6.18 The visualization must always remain responsive.

--------------------------------------------------

7. Patient Management

7.1 Patients can upload reports.

7.2 Oncologists can assign tasks to patients.

7.3 Patients complete tasks by uploading requested information.

7.4 Oncologists can write notes for patients.

7.5 Oncologists can maintain private clinical notes.

7.6 Patients can only view notes intended for them.

7.7 Patients cannot view private clinical notes.

--------------------------------------------------

8. Patient Portal

8.1 Patients can upload reports.

8.2 Patients can view assigned tasks.

8.3 Patients can read oncologist notes intended for them.

8.4 Patients can view their medical timeline.

8.5 Patients can only access information intended for them.

--------------------------------------------------

9. Security

9.1 Patient data must always remain secure.

9.2 Only authorized users may access patient data.

9.3 Every login must be recorded.

9.4 Every important action must be recorded.

9.5 Role-based access control is mandatory.

9.6 Multi-factor authentication must be supported.

--------------------------------------------------

10. User Experience

10.1 The application is experienced as a continuous environment. Users move through it rather than opening unrelated pages.

10.2 Navigation is spatial. Moving deeper zooms in. Moving back zooms out.

10.3 The interface must explain itself. No user should require training.

10.4 The application must never look like a traditional dashboard, an admin panel, a generic EMR or a SaaS template.

10.5 The application must not contain a long sidebar of navigation links or nested menus.

10.6 Users must never be required to remember where a feature is located.

10.7 Every important piece of information must be reachable within three interactions.

10.8 The number of clicks required for any task must be minimized.

10.9 Doctors must never have to search for information. Relevant information reveals itself in context.

10.10 Patients must never feel overwhelmed.

10.11 Every space must answer one question: what is the next thing this user needs.

10.12 The interface must remain clean, readable and easy to understand.

10.13 Important information must always be easy to find.

10.14 The application must look like professional healthcare software suitable for hospitals.

10.15 The application must never look AI generated or low-code generated.

10.16 The design language must remain consistent across the entire environment.

10.17 The interface must never be flashy, childish or game-like.

10.18 Usability must never be sacrificed for visual effect.

--------------------------------------------------

11. Motion

11.1 Animation is a primary interface element, not decoration.

11.2 Every animation must have a purpose.

11.3 Motion must communicate movement through the system.

11.4 Motion must explain where the user is and where they came from.

11.5 Motion must guide attention to what changed.

11.6 Transitions must feel like camera movement.

11.7 Sudden page changes must be avoided.

11.8 Motion must never delay the user or obscure clinical information.

11.9 The operating system reduced motion setting must always be respected, without any loss of functionality or information.

--------------------------------------------------

12. Three-Dimensional Design

12.1 Three-dimensional design is part of the product identity.

12.2 3D is not limited to the Digital Twin.

12.3 Depth, lighting, perspective and spatial interaction may be used anywhere they aid understanding.

12.4 The application should feel physically layered rather than visually flat.

12.5 Decorative 3D is not permitted. Every use of 3D must be meaningful.

12.6 Every 3D view must have an equivalent non-3D representation for accessibility and low-capability devices.

12.7 3D must never reduce the legibility of clinical information.

--------------------------------------------------

13. Performance

13.1 The application must remain responsive.

13.2 Large reports must not slow down the application.

13.3 The Digital Twin must remain smooth during interaction.

13.4 The application should continue functioning even if AI processing is temporarily unavailable.

13.5 Navigation and motion must target 60 frames per second.

13.6 Where a device cannot sustain the target, visual complexity reduces automatically. Clinical meaning must never be reduced.

13.7 A blank screen must never be displayed while loading.

--------------------------------------------------

14. Explainability

14.1 Every AI output must show where it came from.

14.2 Every visualization must be linked to clinical evidence.

14.3 Doctors must always be able to verify AI-generated information.

--------------------------------------------------

15. Timeline

15.1 Every clinical event must appear in chronological order.

15.2 Disease progression must be visible over time.

15.3 Historical information must never be removed.

15.4 Every uploaded report becomes part of the patient's permanent timeline.

15.5 The timeline is also a control. Moving through time moves the entire patient environment through time.

--------------------------------------------------

16. Accessibility

16.1 Every spatial action must have a keyboard equivalent.

16.2 Color must never be the only means of communicating information.

16.3 Contrast, font sizes and spacing must remain readable.

16.4 Every interactive element must have a descriptive label.

16.5 Screen reader users must be able to reach every piece of information available to sighted users.

--------------------------------------------------

17. Development Rules

17.1 New features must follow every rule in this document.

17.2 Existing functionality must never be broken while adding new features.

17.3 Every component should be reusable whenever possible.

17.4 Every space must follow the approved spatial navigation model defined in the UI/UX Design Guide.

17.5 Every implementation should follow the project documentation.

17.6 Code must be production quality. Placeholder code, incomplete components and unresolved TODO comments are not permitted.

17.7 After each completed feature, verify it against every project document before continuing.

--------------------------------------------------

18. Assistant Rules

18.1 The assistant follows the Product Owner's decisions exactly.

18.2 The assistant never changes the project scope unless instructed.

18.3 The assistant never adds features unless instructed.

18.4 The assistant never removes features unless instructed.

18.5 The assistant never redesigns workflows unless instructed.

18.6 The assistant asks questions whenever requirements are unclear.

18.7 The assistant points out only factual implementation consequences when required.

18.8 The assistant keeps every project document consistent with previous approved decisions.

18.9 The assistant writes in simple human language unless instructed otherwise.

18.10 The Ground Rules document is the highest priority document in the project.

18.11 The UI/UX Design Guide is the single source of truth for the spatial model, motion language, 3D rules and vocabulary.
